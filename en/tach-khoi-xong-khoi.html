We build charcoal grills and smokers in a workshop in Nha Be, on the south edge of Ho Chi Minh City. Close to fifty models. Buyers usually open with "which one is the good one", which is the wrong question. These ovens are not one design in six sizes. They are six different mechanisms, and each sends fat, heat and smoke down a different path.

So this is a mechanism map, not a catalogue: where the fat goes, where the heat comes from, what you gain and what you give up.

What is the difference between a smoke-reducing grill and a smoker?

A smoke-reducing grill keeps rendered fat away from live coals, so it makes less of the acrid smoke you get from an open charcoal grill. A smoker does the opposite on purpose: it burns wood slowly to make smoke, then holds that smoke around the meat for hours.

One is getting rid of a smell. The other is building a flavour. Both still smoke. As we put it on our 250L grill page: it still makes smoke, charcoal smoke and fat smoke, just far less of the harsh kind you get when the coals sit right under the meat.

Where does the harsh smoke actually come from?

Not from the charcoal. From the fat. Fat breaks down above roughly 200 °C and live charcoal runs far hotter, so a drip landing on the coals becomes a puff of dirty smoke that settles back onto the meat. Hong Kong's Centre for Food Safety set out that route in Risk Assessment Studies Report No. 14 (2004), and its advice was blunt: keep the meat off direct flame, and keep fat from dripping onto the heat source. That second half is a hardware problem.

The figures come from Lee and colleagues, Food Chemistry 199 (2016): with the drippings removed, the sum of four polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons fell 48 to 89 percent in their trials, and removing the smoke from the food cut it 41 to 74 percent. The main contributor, they concluded, was smoke from fat dripping onto the fire and burning incompletely. That was their laboratory rig, not our ovens. We have never had our grills tested for this.

There is honest evidence on the other side, and it is closer to home than we would like. Sumer and Oz, Foods 12(7):1374 (2023), cooked beef with the heat source in a separate compartment, the smoke kept off the food and the fat kept off the coals — the same idea our ovens are built on — and found no statistically significant difference in total PAH4 or PAH8 against direct barbecuing (p > 0.05). We report it because it is the strongest published test of the principle we sell, and it did not come out our way.

Six mechanisms, and what each one trades away

1. Twin charcoal troughs, food over the gap

Two charcoal troughs sit low with the food between them, and the cooking grate rides high, near the mouth. Heat comes sideways from both fires, and rendered fat falls into the channel between them, into a tray, not onto coals. You get a permanent version of the two-zone fire good grillers build by hand, but no char from directly below, and two fires to feed instead of one. Suits fatty cuts that flare on an open grill: pork belly, chicken quarters, skewers.

2. Three levels with a water pan in the middle

Charcoal at the bottom, a water pan above it, one grate on top. The pan catches every drip and buffers the heat, while the water steams and keeps the meat from drying. The American barbecue world calls this a water smoker. Gentle, forgiving heat, but little searing power, and you top the pan up on long cooks. Suits whole chicken, ribs and shoulder cuts.

3. A V-channel grate that lifts out

A deflector folded into V ridges sits between fire and food. Fat lands on it, runs down the slope and out to both sides into a trough, and after service the deflector lifts out and goes in the sink. Easy cleaning, and small enough for a portable oven, at the cost of some radiant heat. Some models let you pull it out and grill straight over the coals.

4. Two grate levels, two heat zones

The lower grate sits close to the coals and sears. The upper sits well above it and holds or cooks slowly. A vent in the lid and a variable-speed fan set the draft, so the bottom runs hard while the top idles. Two jobs at once during service, but somebody has to keep moving food.

5. An offset firebox

The fire lives in its own box on the side. Heat and smoke cross the cooking chamber and leave through a stack at the far end, so food never sits over flame and drips land on steel. Real wood smoke and a chamber you can hold low for hours, in exchange for feeding the fire every 45 to 60 minutes and learning the gradient, since the end near the firebox runs hotter. Suits brisket, beef ribs and sausage. Start here: offset smoker brisket for beginners.

6. Hybrid: one body, two ways of running it

Hybrid on our labels does not mean two fuels. As our Hybrid 125L page puts it, you change how you run the oven, not what you burn. Open it up for a hot fast cook; shut it down, cut the air, add wood, and the same body works low and slow. Two styles from one footprint, though a dedicated offset will still out-smoke it. Every Hybrid we build runs charcoal or wood. Our one gas-fired oven is the Gas Smoker.

Smoke-Reducing 85L

Frequently asked questions

Do these grills eliminate smoke?

No. Charcoal makes smoke and fat makes smoke. What the design changes is how much fat reaches live coals, which is where the harsh smoke comes from. Anyone promising a charcoal grill that makes none is selling a word, not a mechanism.

Why does my charcoal grill smoke so much?

Three usual causes: fat dripping onto the coals, not enough air so the fuel smoulders and burns incompletely, and sugary marinade burning on the grate. The first is hardware. The other two are technique.

Do all your models work the same way?

No. Six mechanisms run through the range: twin charcoal troughs, a water-pan stack, a removable V-channel grate, zoned grate levels, an offset firebox, and a hybrid body that grills open and smokes closed.

Is the Hybrid a gas grill?

No. Every Hybrid we build runs charcoal or wood. Hybrid means running one oven two ways, grilling and smoking, not two fuels. The one gas oven in our range is the Gas Smoker.

Do the grills need electricity?

Some do, for the air fan alone: 12 V DC 2 A or a Type-C power bank, which we do not supply. The offset smokers and the Pantina Smoker are drafted by hand, through the air intake and the stack valve.

Do you have CE, UKCA or ISO certification?

No. We hold none of them, and no oven of ours has been tested to an emissions or food-contact standard. If your market requires it, that testing has to happen and we will work with you on it.

Can you build these to our drawing?

Yes. We cut our own sheet in-house on a laser that takes plates up to 1250 × 2500 mm, we work from DXF, and we prototype in steel rather than in renderings. Grate spacing, chamber length, vent position and shelves are normal changes.

What is your minimum order quantity?

We do not publish one. We will build a single sample so you can burn it, cook on it and take it apart, with freight at your cost. Better you argue with a real oven than with a PDF.

The short version

Fat landing on live coals is what makes barbecue smoke harsh. Six mechanisms attack that in six ways, and picking the wrong one is how people end up disappointed by a well-built oven. Both families still smoke. Match the mechanism to the food and to the shift you run, not to the litre count on the label.
